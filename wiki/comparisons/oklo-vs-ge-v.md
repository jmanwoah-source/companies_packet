# OKLO vs GEV — SMR Binary vs Grid-Equipment Cash Flow

## Thesis at a Glance
- OKLO: pure SMR developer (Aurora microreactor); revenue near-zero, optionality is licensing + first-deployment contracts.
- GEV: incumbent grid-equipment + nuclear-services giant with a real order book; the "sell shovels" play on the same data-center power buildout.

## Financial Snapshot
- GEV: market cap / backlog verify next run (tracxn + FMP; backlog is the key multiple input).
- OKLO: market cap verify next run; NRC licensing status is the primary fundamental variable.
- Both: order-book/contract detail verify next run via 10-Q/10-K.

## Strategic Comparison
- Business model: OKLO = build-the-reactor (long lead times, binary NRC standard design approval) vs GEV = sell-the-equipment (transformers, turbines, nuclear services — recurring, multi-vendor).
- Cash flow: GEV has installed revenue and margins today; OKLO's cash flow is negative until first commercial deployment.
- Moat: OKLO's is regulatory (license standardization) + customer design-wins; GEV's is scale, service network, and 3-5 year equipment lead times that protect pricing.
- Customer overlap: both sell into the same hyperscaler data-center power story — GEV into the grid, OKLO behind-the-meter.

## Edge and Asymmetry
- Convex side: OKLO — a licensing milestone or first hyperscaler deployment contract reprices a pre-revenue name with no downside anchor.
- Defined side: GEV — near-ceiling market cap ($150B policy flag), so the re-rating is multiple compression/expansion, not binary.
- Portfolio construction: OKLO = Track B (high-beta delta); GEV = Track A reference only if it scores >=75 on safety metrics (near_ceiling flag required).

## Catalysts Ahead
1. NRC standard design approval decisions (OKLO's binary).
2. First behind-the-meter SMR deployment contract with a hyperscaler.
3. GEV quarterly backlog prints (the demand leading indicator for the whole pillar).
4. Transformer lead-time announcements (GEV pricing power signal).
