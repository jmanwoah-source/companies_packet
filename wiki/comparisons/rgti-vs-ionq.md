# RGTI vs IONQ — Quantum Hardware Paths: Transmon vs Trapped-Ion

## Thesis at a Glance
- RGTI: superconducting (transmon) processors; smaller market cap, thinner revenue, deeper cash-per-dollar-of-cap burn question.
- IONQ: trapped-ion systems; ~2.9x larger, more revenue, broader commercial program base (DARPA/Navy contracts).

## Financial Snapshot (2026 data, quantummarketcap.com comparison)
- IONQ: ~$16.18B market cap; latest fiscal-year revenue ~$130.02M.
- RGTI: ~$5.49B market cap; latest fiscal-year revenue ~$7.09M.
- Cash runway: the deciding metric for both (who needs to raise next) — verify next run against latest 10-Q.
- Both names are pre-profitability; revenue quality (recurring service vs one-time systems) is the key split.

## Strategic Comparison
- Hardware path: RGTI transmon (mature superconducting fab ecosystem, cryogenics burden) vs IONQ trapped-ion (vacuum/ion optics, longer coherence per qubit, harder manufacturing scale-up).
- Commercial base: IONQ has visible government/defense programs; RGTI's revenue base is smaller and newer.
- Dilution risk: both are cash-burning with large ATM histories — the capital-structure check (Track A/B discipline) applies harder here than anywhere else in the wiki.
- Valuation: IONQ trades at a much higher revenue multiple; RGTI is priced closer to its cash + optionality value.

## Edge and Asymmetry
- Convex side: RGTI — smaller base, any commercial design-win or DARPA-class award reprices violently; but dilution overhang is the counter-risk.
- Defined side: IONQ — larger installed program base; downside is priced into the multiple less.
- Neither fits Track A (safety bar); both are Track B candidates only with a clean capital-structure check (no imminent dilution).

## Catalysts Ahead
1. Next hardware generation announcement (qubit-count/fidelity milestones).
2. Government contract awards (DARPA/Navy pipeline for IONQ; analogous awards for RGTI).
3. ATM/raise activity (the dilution tell — watch green-shoe language in filings).
4. Error-correction milestone (the first "useful quantum" inflection for either path).
